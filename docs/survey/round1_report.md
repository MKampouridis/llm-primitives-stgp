# LLM-authored primitives for trading GP remain unclaimed

No one in the retrieved literature has yet run genetic programming over LLM-generated trading strategies in the sense that extends *Large Language Quant* (Clayman, Kampouridis & Papastylianou, IEEE CIFEr 2026, DOI 10.1109/cifer67845.2026.11692403). That paper has an agentic LLM write mathematically grounded Python strategies, and a portfolio GA only picks one strategy per asset and sets weights. The specific gap is a tree or strongly typed GP whose terminals and functions are LLM-written, mathematically grounded signals, optionally with the LLM growing and pruning that library during the run. The surrounding territory is crowded, though. Between late 2025 and October 2026, at least five systems evolved trading code with an LLM as the mutation operator: ProFiT (GECCO 2026), QuantEvolve, AlgoEvolve, MadEvolve and EvoQuant. One group (Siper, Khalifa, Togelius) already puts a fine-tuned LLM inside a typed-tree trading language, GPTL. LLM-GA (IEEE SMC 2025) already seeds and varies a trading GA with an LLM. Outside finance, PACE (August 2026) keeps an LLM-grown library of code primitives during evolution, and LaSR evolves an LLM concept library alongside GP. So "evolving LLM-written strategies" and "LLM as a GP operator" cannot be claimed as novel. What can be defended is the narrower combination: LLM-authored advanced-mathematics primitives inside STGP, co-evolved with the LLM, and coupled to a portfolio GA. Two caveats limit the claim. All retrieval used web search and fetch, because Consensus and Elicit were unavailable. And several key full texts (ProFiT, the GPTL primitive list, the PACE appendix) were not read.

A note on scoring. The five note files used slightly different closeness scales. The verification file (`closest_work_verification.md`) scores against the stricter target of "GP evolving trading rules over an LLM-generated primitive library". The other files score against "GP evolving LLM-derived trading strategies". As instructed, the verification file wins wherever the files disagree, which lowers ProFiT, AlgoEvolve, MadEvolve, Continuous Program Search and LaSR to 2 or 3. Rows scored only in other files (for example QuantEvolve, LLM-GA, CogAlpha, LLEGO) keep their original, more lenient scores. Their 4s are therefore not directly comparable with the verification file's 2s and 3s. Original scores are shown in brackets wherever the files disagree. ## 1. Verdict: one category is open, two are partly taken, one is done

**(A) LLM-derived primitives as GP terminals/functions: Not found.** No trading paper places LLM-written signals into a GP function or terminal set. The nearest cases are MadEvolve's LLM-grown feature block and, outside finance, LLM-specialised GE grammars and LLM feature generators.

**(B) LLM-seeded GP populations: Partially done.** LLM-GA seeds a trading GA over signal combinations, CogAlpha seeds an LLM-operated alpha GA, and Alpha-GPT applies GP to LLM-proposed alphas. No study was found that seeds typed tree GP for trading rules.

**(C) LLM-in-the-loop GP: Done** for variation and repair: ProFiT, QuantEvolve, AlgoEvolve, MadEvolve, CogAlpha, and GPTL in Time Value of Evolution. The LLM-as-primitive-generator sub-form was not found.

**(D) Hybrids/co-evolution: Partially done.** AlgoEvolve evolves prompts, and PACE and LaSR co-evolve libraries outside finance. Co-evolution of GP programs with an LLM primitive set in trading was not found.

## 3. Closest prior work: six systems a reviewer will raise first

**The Time Value of Evolution and its group lineage (row 8; also rows 35 and 38).** This is the only work found that puts an LLM inside a typed-tree trading GP. Siper, Khalifa and Togelius search over natural-language trading policies. A multitask fine-tuned Qwen3-8B mutates each policy and compiles it into GPTL, a typed tree language whose outputs are Boolean long/short entry and exit signals. An actor-critic lineage-credit method (LVPG) then sets mutation intensity, and the authors report a **+0.394 Sharpe-unit gain in validation best-so-far AUC over 90 paired runs** on hourly ES, silver and 30Y Treasury futures ([arXiv:2608.13297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13297); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13297)). The overlap with the extension is the representation: GPTL is a typed Boolean entry/exit tree, very close to an STGP rule. The difference is where the LLM sits. Here it mutates and compiles; it does not write primitives. The page that was read does not list GPTL's primitives, says nothing about the LLM adding indicators, and tunes only numeric leaves during validation. The group's other papers share the same fixed-vocabulary stance. Continuous Program Search learns a latent space over a fixed four-part trading DSL, with no LLM involved ([arXiv:2602.07659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07659)). In short, this line improves how the search moves through a fixed language, whereas the extension changes what that language is built from.

**ProFiT (row 35).** ProFiT is the peer-reviewed GECCO 2026 anchor for LLM-evolved trading code ([DOI 10.1145/3795095.3805175](https://doi.org/10.1145/3795095.3805175)). From secondary descriptions, LLMs generate and mutate strategy source code. Each backtest report is fed back to the LLM, strategies above a minimum score join the population, and validation is walk-forward. A co-author's self-reported claim is that it beats buy-and-hold in more than 77% of strategy–asset combinations ([search summary via arXiv:2610.03128](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.03128v1); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5889762)). A community reproduction splits the work between an "Analyst" LLM and a "Developer" LLM and adds an LLM repair step driven by tracebacks ([GitHub](https://github.com/JulesLscx/ProFiT)). That reproduction is not by the authors. For the extension, ProFiT means that "evolutionary search over LLM-written trading code with backtest fitness" is already taken at the main GP venue. What it does not have, as far as the secondary sources show, is a GP tree genome or a primitive set. That conclusion is soft, because neither the ACM nor the SSRN full text could be read (both returned 403).

**PACE (row 9).** PACE is the nearest methodological precedent for category D, although it is outside finance and does not use GP. An LLM creates "executable algorithmic primitives", which are Python helper functions, through three prompts: initialise from the task, extract from the best program, and generate something new conditioned on the top-k primitives. At most one primitive is added per generation. Each primitive has a Beta posterior over how often transferring it improves a child over its parent, and Thompson sampling uses these posteriors to choose which primitive to inject. Four LLM operators then insert, replace, refine or cross over primitive calls inside whole programs ([arXiv:2608.07395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07395); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07395)). The extension differs in five ways. First, composition would be done by typed tree GP rather than by the LLM. Second, the primitives are typed financial signals grounded in specific mathematics. Third, the library is pruned and refined, while PACE's main text describes no pruning. Fourth, credit would come from GP usage and fitness attribution rather than from parent-child improvement. Fifth, the domain is trading with a portfolio GA. PACE's evaluation covers Racing Car, Bipedal Walker and TSP variants against EoH, ReEvo and related baselines. It explicitly sets itself apart from Koza-style primitive sets and from DreamCoder/LILO library learning. The last ~40k characters of its HTML, including the appendix, were not read.

**LaSR (row 39).** LaSR is the canonical case of "GP genome plus an LLM-evolved library". It runs classical GP symbolic regression (PySR), and an LLM abstracts natural-language concepts from elite expressions, evolves that concept library over the run, and uses it to bias initialisation, mutation and crossover ([arXiv:2409.09359](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.09359v2); [NeurIPS 2024](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/poster/96212)). It is the closest structural match to the first variant of D. The key difference is that LaSR's library holds **text hints, not executable typed operators**: GP never calls a concept as a function. The domain is equation discovery with near-deterministic fitness, not noisy trading. A reviewer from the GP community will cite LaSR first against any "co-evolving library" claim. The extension's answer has to be that the library members are themselves executable GP primitives.

**AlgoEvolve (row 36).** AlgoEvolve is the closest trading example of the prompt/spec variant of D. In the inner loop, Gemini Flash rewrites Python trading programs, with the top-2 and bottom-2 prior programs as context. In the outer loop, Gemini Pro meta-evolves four-gene "prompt genomes" using elitism, tournament-style selection, uniform crossover and LLM rewriting of genes. The data are NUMIN 5-minute bars for anonymised equities, the fitness is 0.7 × PnL + 0.3 × consistency, and evaluation is walk-forward ([arXiv:2606.26173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26173); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26173)). The method has no primitive library, and GP appears only in related work. The notes disagree on Table 1. The verification file reads it as 5.60 annualised Sharpe for AlgoEvolve against 5.71 for plain inner-loop evolution. The trading-rules file reads 5.60 as the elite's headline figure and 5.08 as the AlgoEvolve row. Both readings agree that **meta-evolution did not beat the simpler inner loop on Sharpe**, and the figures should be re-checked before quoting. The conclusion names "Modular Program Synthesis … evolving discrete, reusable sub-routines" as future work. That is an independent call for the extension's direction, and also a race risk.

**MadEvolve (row 37).** MadEvolve is the finance system nearest to an LLM-grown indicator set. It evolves BTCUSD 1-minute trading systems AlphaEvolve-style, using MAP-Elites, five ring-topology islands and a five-LLM ensemble. In runs 4 and 5 it also evolves a feature function that maps OHLCV to the input matrix of a fixed ridge predictor; that feature set grew **from 3 to 77–79 features** ([arXiv:2605.23007](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23007)). The features are ordinary momentum, volatility, volume and mean-reversion quantities. They live in a single mutable code block tied to one model and one asset and are never reused across strategies. MadEvolve also benchmarks against Claude Code, which matters because *Large Language Quant* generates its strategies with an agentic coding LLM ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23007v1)). The difference from the extension is clear: MadEvolve's features are not first-class GP primitives, not shared across a population of rule trees, and not curated as a library.

Two further works sit just outside these six. **LLM-GA** (row 2) is the only trading GA in which an LLM seeds the population and performs semantic crossover and mutation. Its abstract reports that LLM initialisation raises starting quality by **215%** and that semantic crossover cuts invalid strategies by **83.5%** ([XJTLU](https://scholar.xjtlu.edu.cn/en/publications/llm-guided-evolutionary-strategy-generation-for-quantitative-trad/)). The genes are conventional signals, so it pre-empts B in a GA setting but not in typed GP. **QuantEvolve** (row 1) evolves Zipline strategies on daily OHLCV with a quality-diversity archive and multi-agent LLM roles. It is the closest match to the extension's data regime, but it has no GP and no allocation across its archive ([arXiv:2510.18569](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18569v1)).

## 4. Gap statement: four defensible claims and six that would fail

**G1. Typed GP over an LLM-authored primitive library for trading rules (category A).** No retrieved work uses LLM-written signals as terminals or functions of a tree or strongly typed GP that evolves trading rules. The nearest neighbours are each missing one piece. GPTL (row 8) has a typed trading tree but a fixed vocabulary. MadEvolve (row 37) has LLM-grown features but no GP and no reuse. The Essex STGP and DC-GP baselines (rows 81–83) use TA, DC, sentiment and fundamental terminals with no LLM. Outside finance, LLM-specialised grammars (rows 28–30) and LLM feature generators (rows 31–32) shape a search space but are not typed trading primitives. Three surveys whose reference lists were mined (rows 145–147) report no LLM-generated GP primitive sets in finance or elsewhere ([arXiv:2608.01789](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01789); [arXiv:2608.31041](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.31041)).

**G2. Advanced-mathematics primitive families inside trading GP at all, with or without an LLM.** Targeted searches found no GP or GE trading paper whose primitives include spectral, permutation or transfer entropy, path signatures, Fisher information or Fisher–Rao geometry, Wasserstein distance, or Hurst/fractal measures. The near-misses all stop short of this. Navet & Chen (row 100) use entropy only to explain GP profitability ([NCCU](https://ah.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/78849)). Liu et al. (row 101) use wavelets only for preprocessing. The 2018 Hurst paper (row 102) names GP only as future work ([SCITEPRESS](https://www.scitepress.org/papers/2018/66670/66670.pdf)). Futter et al. (row 21) build signature strategies without any evolution ([arXiv:2308.15135](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.15135)). On the alpha-mining side, every operator library seen is standard price/volume arithmetic ([Hubble](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09601v1)). The evidence for rate-distortion and volatility phase transitions is weaker: these families were not searched specifically, and they simply never surfaced in any result.

**G3. Co-evolution in which GP evolves rule trees while the LLM grows, prunes and refines the typed primitive set using GP usage and fitness attribution, in trading (category D, first variant).** PACE (row 9) grows and credits code primitives but has no GP, no pruning and no finance application. LaSR (row 39) co-evolves a library with GP, but its library holds text concepts rather than callable primitives. AlgoEvolve (row 36) leaves reusable sub-routines to future work. The defensible novelty is the conjunction of typed GP, executable library members, pruning and refinement, and trading. Library co-evolution on its own is not novel.

**G4. Combining the GP layer with a portfolio GA over heterogeneous assets, and seeding STGP from LLM-written strategies.** No paper GA-allocates across a library of LLM-generated timing strategies. QuantEvolve releases an archive but does not allocate across it, and ELfolio, EPH and LLMDE evolve portfolio heuristics or weights rather than per-asset strategy choices (rows 41–43, all [M]). No study was found that seeds strongly typed trading GP with LLM programs. The precedents are G3P seeding for program synthesis (rows 33–34; [Algorithms 17(7):287](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/tao_2024_Algorithms.html)) and a trading GA seeded by an LLM (row 2). These claims are narrower and should be presented as contributions of the system design rather than as headline novelty.

**Claims that would not survive review:**

- "First to evolve LLM-generated trading strategies": ProFiT, QuantEvolve, AlgoEvolve, MadEvolve and CogAlpha (rows 35, 1, 36, 37, 3) already do this.
- "First to use an LLM as a GP mutation or crossover operator": LLM_GP, LLEGO, LMX and ELM (rows 23, 6, 22, 62) already do this. Hemberg et al. even frame their system explicitly as LLM_GP versus conventional GP ([GPEM](https://gpbib.cs.ucl.ac.uk/gp-html/Hemberg_2024_GPEM.html)).
- "First LLM inside a typed trading-tree GP": GPTL does this (row 8).
- "First LLM-maintained primitive library during evolution": PACE does this (row 9).
- "First co-evolving knowledge library alongside GP": LaSR does this (row 39).
- "First to let an LLM define a GP search space": Vella Zarb et al.'s LLM-predicted grammars did this at GECCO 2025 (row 28).

LLM seeding of a trading GA (row 2) and evolving prompts for trading code (row 36) are also taken. A claim that the extension is the first to handle noisy, non-stationary fitness in LLM+GP is risky. It is an inference from the absence of such work in core benchmarks, and the general-methods search did not test it directly ([AdaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20133v1) uses "non-stationary" to mean search dynamics, not drifting markets). Any absolute "first" should be phrased as "to the best of our knowledge", given the search limits in Section 6.

## 5. Threats: one NYU line, one stated roadmap and a fast preprint stream

The most immediate threat is the **Togelius/Khalifa group** (Siper, Khalifa, Soros, Nasir, Azhang, Togelius). It has produced three trading-search papers in 2026 (rows 8, 35, 38). It already has a typed Boolean entry/exit language (GPTL) and a fine-tuned LLM that compiles natural language into GPTL. Letting that LLM extend GPTL's primitive vocabulary would be a short step for them, and would land squarely on G1. Their GPTL primitive list, possibly in an appendix, has not been checked. Any write-up should cite GPTL as the closest representation and state explicitly that its vocabulary is fixed ([arXiv:2608.13297](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13297)).

The second threat is **AlgoEvolve's stated roadmap**. Sharma and Shroff name modular program synthesis with "discrete, reusable sub-routines" as their next step to break the complexity ceiling they observed ([arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.26173)). That is G1/G3 in an LLM-operator framing. The extension can cite it as independent motivation while stressing the typed GP composition that AlgoEvolve lacks. The **PACE authors** (SUSTech and Shenzhen University) are a third risk: applying their primitive library to a financial benchmark would require little new machinery. **MadEvolve's authors**, whose evolved feature pipeline is the nearest finance analogue to an LLM-grown indicator set, are a fourth.

Several other works need a sentence of differentiation each. **QuantEvolve and CogAlpha** are the strongest LLM-evolution baselines in the extension's data regime. **LLM-GA** comes closest on seeding and semantic variation in trading. **LaSR** comes closest on library co-evolution. **Vella Zarb et al. and the GE/GI grammar line** are the precedent for an LLM shaping the GP search space. **FactorMiner's** skill library differs because it serves an agent, not a GP ([survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.31041)). **Hubble** lists formal evolutionary operators as future work ([HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09601v1)). **"Mutation Without Variation"** (row 132) points to a diversity-collapse risk that reviewers may raise against LLM-operator components.

The flow of new work is fast. Many of the relevant preprints appeared between May and October 2026, among them PACE, Time Value of Evolution, EvoQuant, TradeGrad, LLMDE and EvolveTrade. Venues to watch are GECCO 2027, EuroGP/EvoApplications 2027, IEEE CIFEr, ACM ICAIF, CEC and PPSN, plus arXiv cs.NE and q-fin.TR. The general-methods researcher could not access the GECCO 2025 workshop on LLMs for and with evolutionary computation, and it is a likely venue for competing work ([GECCO 2025 tracks](https://gecco-2025.sigevo.org/Tracks)).
