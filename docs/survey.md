# LLM-generated primitives for trading GP: consolidated survey

The extension of *Large Language Quant* (LLQ; Clayman, Kampouridis & Papastylianou, IEEE CIFEr 2026, DOI 10.1109/CIFEr67845.2026.11692403) is no longer novel at the level of its general mechanism. Two papers already do most of it for alpha mining. ALPHAS2, an anonymous ICLR 2027 submission, has an LLM write new executable, typed operators from successful factor programs, adds them to the grammar of a genuine typed tree GP, and feeds the GP's descendants back to the LLM. EvoQuantLab runs a typed tree GP whose terminal set an LLM-assisted outer loop admits, bans and demotes between cycles, using usage and fitness evidence. Using an LLM to seed, mutate, cross over or repair evolving strategies (categories B and C) is also well established, in finance and elsewhere. What the extension can still defend is narrower: (i) operators imported top-down from mathematics outside finance, rather than distilled bottom-up from price/volume factors; (ii) per-asset boolean entry/exit trading rules across asset classes, rather than cross-sectional factors passed to a ranker; (iii) maintenance of the library (pruning and refinement as well as growth) driven by GP usage and fitness; (iv) semantic typing of operators; (v) decomposition of the existing LLQ strategies into recombinable components, tested at several granularities; and (vi) integration with the existing portfolio GA. Each of these claims has to be differentiated explicitly from ALPHAS2 and EvoQuantLab, and from the typed trading-tree work of the Togelius group.

*Method.* Five independent literature surveys (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Undermind, Kimi) were merged. A blind check verified the merged entries against primary records, and the closest papers were read in full. Searches are dated 30–31 October 2026.

## 2. The five closest papers

**1. ALPHAS2 (Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, OpenReview QjycCr3zns; closeness 5).** A three-agent system for cross-sectional alpha mining. A *semantic evolution agent* (DeepSeek) runs an island-model evolution of executable alpha programs generated from financial hypotheses. Results go into an archive capped at 300 factors, ranked by RankICIR and kept diverse by correlation. An *operator construction agent* reads high-scoring factor programs and the current library, and writes new operators: code, a typed signature, a financial rationale and links to source factors. Each operator is checked automatically for syntax, interface compliance, look-ahead, output shape, finite and non-constant values, and scale; failures are repaired or discarded. There are four shape-based interfaces: unary and binary over (dates × stocks) panels, and rolling and pairwise-rolling over trailing windows. The worked example, FlowDecaySkew, is a new numerical routine rather than a copied subtree. A *symbolic evolution agent* runs a genuine typed tree GP (population 1,000, 500 generations) that rebinds the learned operators to new inputs and windows and nests them. The operator set is fixed within a GP stage and grows between stages. GP descendants return to the archive as parents for the next LLM stage; on CSI500, 26 of the 50 final factors came from GP. Evaluation uses CSI500 (test 2021–24) and CSI1000 (test 2022–Jun 2025), with LightGBM and a Qlib TopK/Drop portfolio net of costs. Baselines are copied from other papers rather than rerun, and the ablation is mixed: removing operator construction raises IC (0.0725 vs 0.0701) but lowers IR (1.74 vs 1.92). *Differences from the extension:* its operators are distilled bottom-up from recurring price/volume motifs (VWAP deviation, skewness, ranks, volume confirmation), not imported from non-financial mathematics; its output is cross-sectional stock ranking through LightGBM, not per-asset entry/exit rules across asset classes; its library only grows, with no pruning or usage-based credit; it types by array shape, not by meaning; it does not decompose existing strategies or compare granularities; and it has no portfolio GA and covers Chinese equities only.

**2. EvoQuantLab (Yang, Qian, Chen et al., arXiv:2610.17130, Oct 2026; HKUST/Microsoft/Tsinghua/U. Macau; closeness 4).** A genuine typed tree GP (tournament selection, typed subtree crossover, point mutation, deduplication with "targeted immigrants", a family-stratified elite archive) evolves cross-sectional factor expressions. The LLM performs no per-candidate variation. Terminals are proposed through an LLM-assisted pathway that extracts economic mechanisms from papers, disclosures and analyst reports into "Reference Cards". A proposed terminal is admitted across a cycle boundary only after a pointwise reference check, a prefix-causality test and, in the case study, a pre-registered paired-seed counterfactual. Each active terminal carries an evidence tuple (utilisation, candidate survival, residual contribution, cross-fold stability) and can be active, interaction-only, dormant or banned. An outer LLM reviewer proposes bounded, evidence-cited patches to the terminal set, operator set, budgets and evaluation rules for the next cycle. Outputs are IC-scored factors fed to LightGBM (equities) or an equal-weight, inverse-volatility book (crypto). Out-of-sample periods are about 2.5–3.5 months (CSI 500, S&P 500, hourly crypto); equity results are gross of costs, crypto net of 5 bps. GP hyperparameters are not reported, and the run used 50 LLM calls costing about $9. *Differences:* the terminals behave like named precomputed series, and the paper does not show the LLM writing executable terminal or operator code, nor a mechanism for creating operators; the output is cross-sectional factors, not trading rules; there are no advanced-mathematics primitives ("entropy" appears only as a diversity diagnostic); and the evaluation is short and, for equities, gross of costs. Its terminal lifecycle is the nearest precedent for usage-based pruning, so claim 3 below must rest on executable operators and trading rules, not on lifecycle management as such.

**3. The Time Value of Evolution / GPTL (Siper, Khalifa, Togelius, arXiv:2608.13297, Aug 2026; closeness 3), with ProFiT (Siper, Khalifa, Soros, Nasir, Azhang, Togelius, GECCO 2026, DOI 10.1145/3795095.3805175; closeness 2–3).** The Time Value of Evolution searches over natural-language trading policies. A multitask fine-tuned Qwen3-8B mutates them and compiles them into GPTL, a typed tree trading language with boolean long/short entry and exit outputs. Lineage-Value Policy Gradients assign credit along lineages and set mutation intensity; the paper reports +0.394 Sharpe units in validation best-so-far AUC over 90 paired runs on hourly ES, silver and 30Y Treasury futures. GPTL is the closest *representation* to the extension's STGP rules, but the LLM mutates and compiles; it does not write primitives. The page read did not list GPTL's primitives, and only numeric leaves are tuned during validation. ProFiT, the GECCO prior art for LLM-evolved trading code, is known only from secondary sources: LLMs generate and mutate strategy source code, backtest reports are fed back, strategies above a minimum score join the population, and validation is walk-forward. The same group's Continuous Program Search (arXiv:2602.07659) learns a latent mutation geometry over a fixed four-part trading DSL. *Differences:* this line keeps the trading language's vocabulary fixed and improves how search moves through it, whereas the extension changes what the language is built from. The group is one step from closing the gap (see Section 5).

**4. PACE (Xie, Zheng, Xu et al., arXiv:2608.07395, Aug 2026; SUSTech and Shenzhen University; closeness 3), with Continual Abstraction Discovery (CAD; Siper, Khalifa, Togelius, arXiv:2608.17947, Aug 2026; closeness 3).** PACE keeps a persistent library of LLM-written "executable algorithmic primitives" (Python helper functions). They are created by three prompts: initialise from the task, extract a computation from the best program, or generate something new conditioned on the top-k primitives, with at most one addition per generation. Each primitive has a Beta posterior over how often transferring it improves a child over its parent, and Thompson sampling chooses which to inject. Four LLM operators then insert, replace, refine or cross over primitive calls inside whole programs. No pruning is described in the main text (the appendix was not read), and the tasks are Racing Car, Bipedal Walker and TSP. CAD has an LLM evolve Python programs by mutation and crossover and extracts reusable primitives from high-fitness programs into a run-specific helper module that later programs adopt widely; in a 2×2 design it raised best fitness in all eight comparisons, in game-level generation. *Differences:* in both, the LLM composes primitives; there is no GP. Neither prunes on usage evidence, neither uses typed signatures for composition, and neither is in finance. CAD matters mainly as a threat signal, because its authors also hold GPTL and ProFiT.

**5. Chen & Qi, "LLM-Assisted Semantic Pruning for Genetic Programming-Based Alpha Factor Discovery" (Applied Sciences 2026, DOI 10.3390/app16126231; closeness 3), with LLM-GA (Zhang D., Jiang, Ji et al., IEEE SMC 2025, DOI 10.1109/SMC58881.2025.11343400; closeness 3).** Chen & Qi run a genuine GP over alpha trees on high-frequency crypto data, and use an LLM (DeepSeek) to flag and prune redundant or implausible branches. This is category C (repair and simplification) inside a real GP in finance. LLM-GA is a GA over combinations of technical, fundamental and sentiment signals on Chinese stocks (2020–24). An LLM seeds the initial strategies and performs semantically aware crossover and mutation; its abstract reports that LLM initialisation raises starting quality by 215% and that semantic crossover cuts invalid strategies by 83.5% (only the abstract page was read). *Differences:* neither creates primitives. Chen & Qi's LLM edits trees over a fixed vocabulary, and LLM-GA's genes are conventional signals in a GA rather than typed trees. Together they pre-empt "LLM pruning in alpha GP" and "LLM-seeded, LLM-varied trading GA".

## 3. Defensible claims

Phrase every claim as "to the best of our knowledge" and differentiate it from the named papers.

1. **Top-down, cross-domain mathematical operators, written by an LLM as executable and parameterised code, composed by STGP for trading.** Examples: `SpectralEntropy(series, window)`, `LevyArea(path_x, path_y, window)`, `FisherRao(hist_a, hist_b)`, `W1(window_short, window_long)`. *Differentiate from:* ALPHAS2 (bottom-up operators distilled from price/volume factor motifs); EvoQuantLab (literature-derived terminals, no advanced mathematics); CogAlpha (its "fractal" agent targets roughness and long memory by description only, and its "Fisher" is the Fisher z-transform); InsightSR (precomputed features, non-finance). Even without an LLM, no GP/GE trading paper using entropy, Hurst/fractal measures, signatures, Fisher information or Fisher–Rao, or Wasserstein distance as primitives was found by any survey. The near-misses are Li, Shi & Li 2006 (wavelet-based indicators feeding GP; whether the wavelet scales are executable nodes is unverified), Liu, Ji & Jin 2016 (wavelets as preprocessing only), Navet & Chen 2008 (entropy as a diagnostic), the 2018 Hurst paper (GP only as future work), and Guidetti et al. (Fisher geometry used to prune GP expressions). Path signatures are already used in trading without GP (Futter et al.; Guo, Jin, Kuang et al.).
2. **Per-asset boolean entry/exit trading rules across asset classes (crypto, FX, indices, commodities).** *Differentiate from:* ALPHAS2 and EvoQuantLab (cross-sectional factors passed to LightGBM or a long-short book); GPTL (typed boolean entry/exit trees, but a fixed vocabulary and an LLM only as mutator and compiler); the Essex STGP and DC-GP baselines (no LLM).
3. **Usage- and fitness-driven maintenance of an executable operator library: growth, pruning and refinement between GP cycles, credited by GP usage, survival and contribution.** *Differentiate from:* ALPHAS2 (grow-only library, no credit); EvoQuantLab (evidence-based lifecycle, but over terminals, without LLM-written operator code); PACE (Thompson-sampling credit by parent–child improvement, no pruning, no GP); CAD (non-GP, non-finance); LaSR (text concepts, not callable primitives); InsightSR (features accumulate; no explicit removal).
4. **Semantic typing of operators** (series, distribution/histogram, path, regime state, boolean signal), so that STGP composes, for example, Fisher–Rao distance (histogram × histogram → real) or Lévy area (path × path → real) correctly. *Differentiate from:* ALPHAS2 (four shape-based interfaces); GPTL and the Essex STGP work (typed, but over a fixed vocabulary); Menoita & Silva (vectorial typed GP, no LLM).
5. **Decomposition of LLQ's eight LLM-written strategies into recombinable components, with a matched-budget comparison of granularity** (whole strategies vs indicator series vs parameterised operators). The research question is whether exposing the strategies' internal mathematical building blocks lets GP recombine them better than treating each strategy as an indivisible signal. *Differentiate from:* LLQ itself (whole strategies, selection only); ProFiT, QuantEvolve, AlgoEvolve and MadEvolve (whole-program evolution); ALPHAS2 (distils operators from its own factors but makes no granularity comparison).
6. **Integration with the existing portfolio GA, with multi-asset, walk-forward, net-of-cost evaluation.** This is an empirical and system contribution, not an algorithmic headline. *Differentiate from:* EvoQuantLab (2.5–3.5-month OOS; equities gross of costs); ALPHAS2 (Chinese equities only; copied baselines); QuantEvolve (an archive, but no allocation across it); ELfolio, EPH and LLMDE (evolve portfolio heuristics or weights, all unverified).

### Claims that will not survive review

- "First LLM + GP" (LLM_GP, LLEGO, LMX, ELM and many others).
- "First evolutionary optimisation of LLM-generated trading strategies or factors" (ProFiT, QuantEvolve, CogAlpha, AlgoEvolve, MadEvolve, LLM-GA).
- "First LLM-seeded GP population" (Kobilov & Lan; InsightSR; MaOG3P).
- "First LLM mutation, crossover or repair for alpha or trading" (ProFiT, CogAlpha, both EvoAlpha papers, LineageEvo, AlphaMemo, Chen & Qi).
- "First LLM inside a typed trading-tree GP" (Time Value of Evolution / GPTL).
- "First evolving primitive vocabulary for financial GP" (EvoQuantLab; ALPHAS2).
- "First to have an LLM write typed operators for GP" (ALPHAS2).
- "First co-evolution of LLM factor search and the GP primitive set" (ALPHAS2).
- "First GP-to-LLM feedback loop in alpha mining" (ALPHAS2).
- "First LLM-maintained primitive library during evolution" (PACE; CAD).
- "First co-evolving knowledge library alongside GP" (LaSR).
- "First to let an LLM define the GP search space" (Vella Zarb et al., GECCO 2025).
- "First evolution of specifications that an LLM implements" (TreEvo; AlgoEvolve; AlphaSchema).
- "First use of path signatures in trading" (Futter et al.; Guo, Jin, Kuang et al.).
- "First to handle noisy, non-stationary fitness in LLM+GP": untested, so too risky to claim.

### Suggested contribution statement

> We extend Large Language Quant from selecting among whole LLM-written strategies to recombining their parts. An LLM writes executable, parameterised and semantically typed operators drawn top-down from mathematics outside finance, such as information geometry, optimal transport, rough paths and spectral entropy, together with operators decomposed from the original LLQ strategies. A strongly typed GP composes these operators into per-asset entry and exit rules, which feed the existing portfolio GA. Between GP cycles, the LLM grows, prunes and refines the operator library using evidence of GP usage, survival and fitness contribution. Unlike recent alpha-mining systems that grow an operator set bottom-up from price/volume factors (ALPHAS2) or revise a terminal set from literature (EvoQuantLab), we target trading rules across asset classes and maintain executable operators in both directions. We isolate each component's contribution with matched-budget ablations of granularity, library maintenance and LLM involvement, including an expert-written-operator control, and evaluate walk-forward, net of costs, on a post-cutoff test period.

## 4. Recommended experimental design

**Held fixed across all arms:** the asset universe, data splits, backtest harness, cost model, portfolio GA, and the total budget, with LLM tokens and GP evaluations matched and reported per arm.

| Arm | Description | Question answered | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Original LLQ pipeline (whole strategies, portfolio GA selection and weights) | Reference point | Undermind |
| 2a | STGP with whole LLQ strategies as boolean terminals, no LLM in the loop | Does recombining whole strategies help? | ChatGPT (decomposition) |
| 2b | STGP with precomputed LLQ indicator series as terminals | Does exposing internal signals help? | ChatGPT; Undermind arm 2 |
| 2c | STGP with parameterised LLQ operators (rebindable to inputs and windows) | Does operator-level granularity help beyond 2a and 2b? | ChatGPT |
| 3 | Frozen library: the LLM proposes cross-domain operators once; STGP runs over them | Value of LLM-authored operators without maintenance | Undermind |
| 4 | Maintained library: the LLM grows, prunes and refines operators between cycles, gated by evidence. Sub-ablation 4-grow (growth only) vs 4-full (grow + prune + refine) | Value of maintenance, and of pruning specifically | Undermind; claim 3 |
| 5 | Fixed grammar with LLM tree operations (seeding, semantic mutation, pruning in the style of Chen & Qi) | Is the LLM better used as an operator than as a library author? | Undermind |
| 6 | Expert-written control: the same mathematical operators as arm 3/4, implemented by hand | Does the LLM add anything beyond implementation convenience? | ChatGPT judgement |
| 7 | Comparator, where feasible: (a) ALPHAS2-style bottom-up distillation of operators from high-fitness trees; (b) EvoQuantLab-style terminal-only lifecycle | Is top-down cross-domain sourcing, and operator-level maintenance, better than the published mechanisms? | This report |
| Baselines | Essex STGP/DC-GP with technical-analysis terminals; buy-and-hold | Context | Claude survey |

Arms 7a and 7b would be reimplementations from the papers' descriptions; ALPHAS2 is anonymous and under review, and the availability of code for either was not checked.

**Leakage and robustness controls**

- **Prefix invariance.** Appending future data must not change any value already computed for the past. Test every operator automatically before admission, as EvoQuantLab and ALPHAS2 also do.
- **Versioned primitive contracts.** Record each operator's types, lookback, earliest availability and missing-data behaviour. Every modified implementation gets a new identity, so that results are attributable to exact code versions.
- **Matched LLM and GP budgets, with full cost reporting.** Report tokens, wall-clock time, LLM calls, rejected programs, repairs and failures per arm.
- **Pre-registered primary outcome.** Fix one primary metric, for example out-of-sample, net-of-cost portfolio Sharpe over the walk-forward test period, before running the experiments. Treat everything else as secondary.
- **Post-cutoff test period and LLM contamination.** Record model names and versions. Include a test period that starts after the LLM's training cutoff, because the LLM may have seen the historical periods.
- **Cost sensitivity.** LLQ's transaction-cost sample (Oct 2025–Jan 2026) postdates its historical tests, so report results across a range of cost assumptions.
- **Seeds versus market periods.** LLQ's 50 GA seeds measure optimiser variance on the same market years, not independent market histories. Report variance across market periods (walk-forward windows, asset classes) separately from variance across seeds. Add a selection-aware robustness test, such as a matched-budget block-permutation search.
- **Lifecycle metrics.** Report operator admission, survival, reuse and transfer across assets and cycles.

**Motivating citations for using GP rather than the LLM for combinatorial search**

- *Mutation Without Variation* (Gurkan, Stonedahl & Wilensky, GECCO '26 LLM4EC workshop, arXiv:2606.05408). Without selection, iterated LLM mutation collapses onto a few program structures: in 87% of chains, more than 93% of mutations revisit an earlier structural skeleton. Classical subtree mutation does not converge in this way.
- *AlgoEvolve* (Sharma & Shroff, arXiv:2606.26173), "Alpha Silence". Under a fixed prompt, LLM mutation drifted into conservative filters and small threshold tweaks ("syntactic over-smoothing") until programs placed no trades at all on the validation assets.
- *CogAlpha* (ACL 2026). Its generation and judging prompts **forbid nested loops; strongly prefer vectorised operations**. Single loops remain allowed, but this kind of constraint limits genuinely iterative mathematics such as Sinkhorn/optimal-transport or geodesic computations. That supports writing such operators once, carefully, as library members, rather than regenerating them inside every candidate.

*Conventions.*
- **Closeness** uses the judgement file's score where it gives one, otherwise the Claude survey's. The Claude survey's scores came from note files with different, sometimes more lenient scales, so rows scored only there (for example EvoAlpha at 4, LLEGO at 4) are not strictly comparable with the judged scores. Where the judgement gave a range for a group, scores outside the range were brought inside it (EFS 4 → 3; Futter et al. → 1–2). "3–4" and "2–3" are scores given as ranges. An asterisk (*) means the score was assigned by the compiler (the Claude survey, or this report for 2* and 1* items new here) rather than by a note file or the judgement. "n/s" means not scored.
- **Found by:** C = Claude, G = ChatGPT, Ge = Gemini, U = Undermind, K = Kimi, F = follow-up check by the judging agents. "K mention" or "Ge (text)" means the paper appeared only in that survey's prose, not in its table.
- **Verification status:** *Verified* means confirmed against primary records (arXiv API, Crossref, ACL Anthology, PMLR, NeurIPS/ICLR sites, GitHub) in round 2; *Corrected* means it exists but at least one bibliographic detail was wrong in some survey and is corrected here; *Unverified* means not covered by the round-2 check and keeps the Claude survey's source tag ([S] = retrieved in the Claude session, [M] = from memory, [S-cite] = seen only in another paper's reference list); *Industry report* means a non-peer-reviewed sell-side report.
- "n/r" = not recorded in the inputs; "n/v" = not verified. "Full title not recorded in inputs" marks works whose short name only was recorded; take the full title from the primary record before citing.

**Lesson for the methods section.** No single tool was sufficient. The two papers that decide the verdict were each found by only some tools: EvoQuantLab by ChatGPT and Undermind, ALPHAS2 by Gemini and Kimi. Any one survey alone would have produced a wrong verdict, either overclaiming A and D (Claude) or missing the GECCO prior art that a reviewer is most likely to raise (ChatGPT). Citation accuracy varied independently of coverage: the tool with the most important unique find (Gemini) also had the most bibliographic errors, so every citation should be checked against a primary record.

## 8. Limitations and next steps

**Limitations**
- **ProFiT full text not read.** The ACM and SSRN versions returned 403; its method is known only from secondary sources. Read the GECCO PDF before submission.
- **GPTL primitive list not checked.** It may be in the appendix of arXiv:2608.13297. Confirm that the vocabulary is fixed and does not already include advanced-mathematics primitives.
- **ALPHAS2 is under double-blind review and anonymous.** Cite it as "Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, OpenReview QjycCr3zns" until it is de-anonymised. Automated access to OpenReview was blocked, so it was read from a PDF the user supplied.
- **EvoQuantLab ambiguities.** The paper does not say whether its LLM ever writes terminal code, how LLM hypotheses seed the GP, or what "dynamic operator retrieval across cycles" involves. Read the latest version yourself.
- **Huatai reports unverified.** The two "GPT Factor Factory" sell-side reports (2024) were reported by Kimi only and not independently checked.
- **Subscription databases not searched:** Scopus, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore, ACM DL; nor the GECCO, EuroGP and EvoApplications 2026 tables of contents, Google Scholar, DBLP or the gpbib keyword index.
- **Partly read sources:** the PACE appendix, the second half of MadEvolve, part of CogAlpha's agent hierarchy (since covered by the round-2 read), and two-thirds of arXiv:2509.08269. AlgoEvolve's Table 1 figures were read differently by two Claude notes (5.60 vs 5.71 or 5.08); recheck before quoting.
- **Unverified rows.** Rows marked Unverified, especially [M] and [S-cite] items, need checking against primary records before they are cited. Li, Shi & Li 2006 needs a full-text check of whether its wavelet scales are executable GP nodes.
- **Consensus pass pending.** Consensus's free quota resets after 1 November 2026, so one more index pass is possible.
