## s01 run_information_reversion (information_theory)

**Hypothesis.** Daily return paths carry two kinds of exploitable information. (1) When the short up/down string becomes compressible (its binary entropy falls well below 1 bit) on the downside, the run tends to revert, so a long entered after a low-entropy down-run earns a reversal premium; a low-entropy up-run is the symmetric signal to take profit. (2) When the risk 'energy' (squared returns) is spread evenly across recent days (high energy entropy) the market is in a diffusive regime in which the risk premium is earned with shallow drawdowns, whereas a concentration of energy in few days signals shocks to step aside from. Holding through the diffusive regime is only worthwhile when the long-run return series carries positive information against a zero-drift model.

**Definition.** Let r_t be daily log returns.
Run information (W_s = 10): p_t = (1/W_s) sum_{i<W_s} 1[r_{t-i} > 0], H2(p) = -p log2 p - (1-p) log2(1-p), x_t = sign(p_t - 1/2) (1 - H2(p_t)) in [-1, 1] bits.
Energy entropy (W_e = 20): e_i = r_{t-i}^2 / sum_{j<W_e} r_{t-j}^2, h_t = -sum_i e_i ln e_i / ln W_e in [0, 1] (Shannon entropy of the energy distribution over time; its Renyi-2 counterpart is ln W_e - ln(sum r^4 / (sum r^2)^2 * W_e)).
Drift information (n = 252): d_t = sign(mu) n D_KL(N(mu, s^2) || N(0, s^2)) = sign(mu) n mu^2 / (2 s^2) nats, mu and s^2 the sample mean and variance of the last n returns.
Adaptive thresholds: q_hi, q_lo = 50% and 20% quantiles of h over the last 126 bars.
Entry_t = [x_t < -0.1] OR ([h_t > q_hi] AND [x_t < 0.1] AND [d_t > 0]).
Exit_t  = [x_t > 0.2] OR ([h_t < q_lo] AND [x_t > -0.1]) OR ([d_t <= 0] AND [x_t > 0]).
Long from entry until exit (exit wins ties); the harness applies the one-bar delay and costs.

**Novelty (agent's claim).** Uses information quantities as both the timing and the regime variables: low-entropy (compressible) sign runs are traded as reversal/take-profit events, while the entropy of the temporal energy distribution (a scale-free, kurtosis-like 'shock concentration' measure, applied in time rather than across wavelet scales) decides whether to hold between runs, and a KL drift-evidence term gates that holding leg. Thresholds are expressed in bits/nats or as rolling quantiles, so the rule transfers across assets without rescaling.

**Limitations.** Long-only: on assets without drift (most currency pairs) the holding leg adds little and results are near zero or negative. Turnover is moderate (about 7 round trips per year), so high-cost markets are penalised. Development results were noisy across nearby parameter settings (objective roughly 0.05-0.13), so the edge is modest. The 252-bar drift window adds a one-year warm-up.

## s02 ordinal_efficiency_regime (information_theory)

**Hypothesis.** Risk premia are earned with shallow drawdowns while the return process is informationally efficient, i.e. while the rank-order (ordinal) dynamics of consecutive returns are close to those of an i.i.d. process and the permutation entropy is high. When ordinal structure emerges - a few rank patterns of consecutive returns become over-represented, so the permutation entropy drops into the low tail of its own recent history - the market is in a structured, less efficient state (herding, forced flows, crisis dynamics) in which drawdown risk is concentrated. A long position is therefore held through high-entropy regimes, closed when ordinal entropy collapses, and re-opened once the entropy has recovered above its one-year median.

**Definition.** Let r_t be the daily log return. For embedding dimension m=4 (delay 1) map each vector (r_{t-3}, r_{t-2}, r_{t-1}, r_t) to its ordinal pattern pi_t in S_4 (the permutation that sorts it; ties broken by order of occurrence). Over the last N=40 patterns form the empirical distribution p_t(pi) = #{s in (t-N, t] : pi_s = pi} / N and the normalised permutation entropy H_t = -sum_pi p_t(pi) ln p_t(pi) / ln(m!), with H_t in [0,1] and H_t -> 1 for an i.i.d. continuous process (all m! = 24 patterns equiprobable). Let Q_t(q) be the q-quantile of {H_s : s in (t-252, t]}. Entry condition at t: H_t > Q_t(0.5). Exit condition at t: H_t < Q_t(0.2). The harness holds a long position from an entry to the next exit (flat otherwise) with its own one-bar execution delay. Total lookback: 44 + 252 bars.

**Novelty (agent's claim).** Unlike run_information_reversion (binary entropy of up/down strings, entropy of the distribution of squared-return energy across days, and a zero-drift information test), this strategy rests on the Bandt-Pompe ordinal symbolisation: it measures the Shannon entropy of the distribution of rank-order permutations of consecutive returns. This statistic is invariant to scale and to any monotone transformation of returns, so it is not a volatility, energy-concentration or drift measure; it captures the dependence structure (efficiency) of the return process. It is used as a regime filter relative to its own one-year distribution, with no reversal entries and no drift test.

**Limitations.** The edge found in development is small and noisy: the objective was 0.156, against about -0.04 for a near buy-and-hold reference, and was sensitive to the entropy window (a 60-bar window scored about -0.19, partly because of too few trades). Because thresholds are relative quantiles, the strategy is always out about 35-45% of the time even in markets that never become structured, which costs drift in strong bull markets. As a long-only rule it cannot profit in assets without a risk premium (most currency pairs scored about -0.2 to -0.35, largely from drawdowns while invested). Equity indices with persistent declines are not protected, because ordinal entropy is direction-blind. With 40 bars and 24 patterns, the entropy estimate is biased downwards and noisy; only the relative quantile makes it usable. Trade frequency is about 3-4 entries a year, close to the 2-per-year minimum on short or quiet series.

## s03 context_tree_rank_prediction (information_theory)

**Hypothesis.** Medium-horizon return paths carry sequential information - momentum in some markets, reversal in others, patterns that change over time - that no fixed model captures for every market. A universal source coder learns this information without choosing a model order: the context-tree-weighting (CTW) mixture over all variable-order Markov models up to depth D is, in code length, within O(k log n) bits of the best context-tree model in hindsight, and its coding distribution is therefore a minimax-optimal sequential predictor (prediction = compression). The strategy codes 10-day block returns with a maximum-entropy quantiser (equiprobable rank cells relative to the recent distribution). This removes the level of drift and volatility, so the coder can only exploit predictive (mutual) information between past and future block ranks. It holds a long position while the coder's predictive distribution expects the next block to rank above the middle. Where block ranks are unpredictable, the coder's forecasts hover around the middle rank and the position switches frequently but is held only about 30-50% of the time; where they are predictable, the predictor's regret vanishes and the bets follow the learned structure.

**Definition.** Let r_t be daily log returns, h = 10 (horizon), B_t = sum_{i=0}^{h-1} r_{t-i} the h-day block return. Maximum-entropy quantisation: with q_j(s) the j/M quantiles (j=1..M-1, M = 4) of {B_{s-Q+1},...,B_s} (Q = 60), the symbol is x_s = #{j : B_s >= q_j(s)} in {0,..,M-1}, i.e. the equiprobable cell of B_s. Context of symbol x_s: c_s = (x_{s-h}, x_{s-2h}, ..., x_{s-Dh}), D = 3 (non-overlapping preceding blocks). Training set at t: pairs (x_s, c_s) for s in (t-W, t], W = 252. For every node u of the M-ary context tree of depth <= D, n_u(a) counts symbol a after context suffix u. Krichevsky-Trofimov estimate: P_KT(n) = Gamma(M/2) prod_a Gamma(n(a)+1/2) / (Gamma(1/2)^M Gamma(N+M/2)). CTW: P_w(u) = P_KT(n_u) at depth D, otherwise P_w(u) = 1/2 P_KT(n_u) + 1/2 prod_{b} P_w(bu). Sequential predictive distribution for the next block (t+1..t+h), whose context is c* = (x_t, x_{t-h}, ..., x_{t-(D-1)h}): p_t(a) = P_w^{root}(counts + a on path c*) / P_w^{root}(counts), normalised over a. Signal: centred expected rank S_t = sum_a (a - (M-1)/2) p_t(a). Rule: F_t = mean(S_{t-4}, ..., S_t) (overlapping bets on the 10-day horizon); entry when F_t > 0, exit when F_t < 0. Lookback = W + D h + h + Q + 4 = 356 bars.

**Novelty (agent's claim).** Earlier strategies from this field used entropy *levels* as regime filters: binary entropy of a short up/down string plus energy entropy (run_information_reversion), and permutation entropy relative to its own history (ordinal_efficiency_regime). This strategy uses no entropy level or regime threshold. It applies universal source coding as a predictor: the Bayesian context-tree-weighting mixture over all variable-order Markov models, with Krichevsky-Trofimov estimators, on a maximum-entropy quantisation of multi-day block returns. It trades the coder's sequential predictive distribution (the probability it would use to compress the next symbol). Each market learns its own dependency structure (momentum, reversal, or none) with no assumed model order.

**Limitations.** Long warm-up (356 bars) before the first decision. Overlapping block symbols make the KT counts over-confident (the effective sample is roughly W/h rather than W). The equiprobable quantiser deliberately discards the drift, so in steady bull markets exposure stays around 35-45% and much of the equity risk premium is forgone. Several markets have little sequential information at a 10-day horizon (most FX pairs here, weak equity indices), where results are about zero or negative. Turnover is moderate (roughly 5-14 entries per year), so very high-cost markets are penalised. On the development data the median objective was sensitive to discrete choices (number of levels, quantisation window, averaging span), with roughly ±0.1 swings, so out-of-sample performance should be expected to be noticeably lower than the best development value.

## s04 min_discrimination_forecast (information_theory)

**Hypothesis.** Whether holding a market is rewarded depends on its current state (recent drift and volatility), but the sign and shape of that dependence differ across markets and drift over time (momentum here, rebounds after stress there, leverage effects in equities, flight-to-safety rallies in others). Kullback's principle of minimum discrimination information gives the least-biased way to condition a market's own recent history on today's state. Among all re-weightings of the past year's (state, forward return) scenarios whose average state equals today's, choose the one closest to the empirical distribution in relative entropy, i.e. the one that adds the fewest nats of information beyond the state constraint. The expected forward return under this I-projection is a model-free, market-specific forecast. A long position is held while that forecast is positive and closed when it turns negative. The strategy therefore steps aside in states that the market's own history associates with losses (e.g. high-volatility sell-offs in equities) and stays invested where similar states were rewarded.

**Definition.** Daily log returns r_t. State of day i (s = state_window = 20): x_i = (m_i/v_i, log v_i), with m_i = (1/s) sum_{k=0}^{s-1} r_{i-k} and v_i = sqrt((1/s) sum_{k=0}^{s-1} r_{i-k}^2). Outcome of day i (h = horizon = 10): y_i = sum_{k=1}^{h} r_{i+k}. At bar t the scenario set is S_t = {i : t-h-N+1 <= i <= t-h} (N = scenarios = 252, using at least 60 while history is shorter), so every outcome y_i is known at t. The states are standardised over S_t (z_i = (x_i - mean)/sd) and d_i = z_i - z_t. Minimum discrimination information (I-projection, Kullback 1959; Csiszar 1975): q* = argmin_q KL(q || u) subject to E_q[d] = 0, with u uniform on S_t. Its solution is the exponential family q_i(theta) = exp(theta.d_i) / sum_j exp(theta.d_j), where theta minimises the convex dual log sum_i exp(theta.d_i). The constraint is softened with a Gaussian prior on theta (ridge lambda = 1): theta* = argmin_theta [log sum_i exp(theta.d_i) + (lambda/2)|theta|^2], solved by Newton's method. Equivalently, q* is the MAP exponential tilt and KL(q*||u) stays finite when today's state lies outside the historical hull. Signal: F_t = sum_i q*_i y_i / sd_{S_t}(y), the minimum-relative-entropy forecast of the h-day forward return, in standard-deviation units. Rule: entry when F_t > 0, exit when F_t < 0 (position from entry until exit).

**Novelty (agent's claim).** The earlier strategies in this field measure entropy or complexity of the return process itself: binary/energy entropy of runs, permutation entropy, and context-tree-weighting compression of block ranks. This strategy measures no entropy of returns at all. It uses relative entropy as an inference principle: the minimum-discrimination-information (I-projection / entropy-pooling) update of the empirical scenario distribution, conditioned on today's drift/volatility state, gives a nonparametric conditional forecast of the forward return. There is no fixed model of how state maps to return. The exponential tilt learns, per market and over a rolling year, whether e.g. high volatility or recent losses preceded gains or losses. The ridge-regularised dual keeps the update finite for states never seen before.

**Limitations.** The forecast is a weighted mean of about 250 overlapping 10-day outcomes, so it is noisy. In driftless markets (most currency pairs) it hovers around zero, giving about 40-50% exposure with little edge. It learns only from the last year, so it adapts late to new regimes and can be wrong right after a break (e.g. the first sell-off after a long calm). Overlapping outcomes and the shared state window make the effective sample much smaller than 252. Because the rule has no hysteresis, trade frequency depends on how often the forecast changes sign. Hysteresis was tested and made entries too rare for the minimum-trading requirement, so turnover is moderate (about 2-10 round trips a year). The roughly 90-bar warm-up and the short one-year memory make early results depend on the first year of data. Results on the development data were sensitive to the horizon, state window and ridge.

## s05 hyperbolic_dislocation_reversal (information_geometry)

**Hypothesis.** On the statistical manifold of Gaussian return models, equipped with the Fisher-Rao metric (a hyperbolic half-plane), the model fitted to the last two weeks of returns wanders around the model fitted to the last six months. A large geodesic excursion towards lower drift that is NOT accompanied by a large excursion along the scale (volatility) direction is mostly sampling noise / liquidity-driven overshoot and tends to relax back towards the long-run model, giving a positive expected return to a long position held until the drift side of the excursion closes. Excursions with a large scale component are regime breaks (volatility shocks) where the long-run model is no longer a valid anchor, so they are not traded.

**Definition.** Gaussian fits on trailing windows of daily log returns r: (mu_s, s_s) over n=10 bars, (mu_l, s_l) over L=120 bars. Fisher-Rao distance on the univariate normal manifold (metric ds^2=(dmu^2+2 dsigma^2)/sigma^2, a scaled Poincare half-plane): d_FR = sqrt2 * arccosh(1 + ((mu_s-mu_l)^2/2 + (s_s-s_l)^2)/(2 s_s s_l)). Dislocation signal D_t = sign(mu_s-mu_l) * d_FR * sqrt(n) (geodesic distance in units of the sampling standard error ~ 1/sqrt(n)). Scale signal V_t = sqrt2*ln(s_20/s_252), the signed Fisher-Rao length of the pure-scale geodesic between the 20- and 252-bar volatility models. Entry condition E_t = [D_t < -1] AND [V_t < 0.5]. Exit condition X_t = [D_t > 0] OR [E_tau false for all tau in (t-10, t]] (time stop: a position is not kept more than 10 bars after the last dislocation signal). The harness holds a long position from an entry until an exit.

**Novelty (agent's claim).** Short-term reversal is usually measured with z-scores of price or return. Here the trigger is the hyperbolic (Fisher-Rao) geodesic distance between two fitted return distributions, which jointly prices drift and scale changes in an invariant way, and the decomposition of the manifold displacement into its drift-signed total length and its pure-scale geodesic length is used to separate tradeable dislocations from volatility regime breaks. During development, gating on the scale component in both directions was tested: dips in calm regimes (V<0) lost money, unbounded scale excursions added drawdown, and the bounded gate V<0.5 worked best.

**Limitations.** Gaussian fits on 10 bars are noisy, so the signal is close to a statistical test with a modest edge; the development objective was only marginally positive (about +0.01) and nearby variants scattered roughly +/-0.1, so performance is fragile. The edge comes mainly from equity indices and commodities; on currency pairs (no drift, symmetric) long-only reversal had roughly zero Sharpe with real drawdowns. It does badly in persistent bear markets, where dips keep extending. Turnover is about 20-30 per year, so it is sensitive to trading costs. The 252-bar warm-up of the scale signal means it does not trade in the first year of data.

## s06 dispersive_geodesic_relaxation (information_geometry)

**Hypothesis.** A volatility shock throws the short-horizon return law far from the long-run law on the dispersive (high-scale) side of the Gaussian statistical manifold. While the shock is still expanding, the risk of continued losses is high; once the short-horizon model starts travelling back along the manifold towards the long-run model (negative radial geodesic velocity), the shock is being absorbed and the market tends to pay the elevated risk premium demanded during the shock (post-shock rebound / volatility mean-reversion). A long position held only during this inward motion, and abandoned at the first outward step, harvests that premium with short holding periods, low exposure and small drawdowns. Because Fisher-Rao distance is invariant to rescaling returns, the same thresholds apply to any asset class.

**Definition.** Let r_t be daily log returns. Short model: theta_t = (mu_t, sigma_t), sample mean and standard deviation of r over the last w = 20 bars. Anchor: theta^A_t = (mu^A_t, sigma^A_t) over the last L = 252 bars. On the Gaussian manifold with Fisher metric ds^2 = (dmu^2 + 2 dsigma^2)/sigma^2 (sqrt(2) x Poincare half-plane in (mu/sqrt2, sigma)), the geodesic distance is d(theta1, theta2) = sqrt(2) * arccosh(1 + ((mu1-mu2)^2/2 + (sigma1-sigma2)^2)/(2 sigma1 sigma2)). ## s07 simplex_fisher_rao_stationarity (information_geometry)

## s08 mirror_divergence_reversal (information_geometry)

**Hypothesis.** The sign flip r -> -r acts on any family of return laws. Its fixed points form the submanifold of direction-symmetric laws, where up and down moves are equally frequent and equally large. When the recent law sits significantly on the loss side of its mirror image (losses more frequent and/or larger than gains, beyond sampling noise), the imbalance is mostly transient one-sided order flow: forced selling or inventory pressure that liquidity providers absorb at a premium. It then decays back towards the symmetric submanifold. A long position opened when the loss-side divergence is large, and held until the law crosses back to the gain side, collects that premium. The divergence does not change when returns are rescaled (r -> c r), so the same thresholds apply to every asset class.

**Definition.** Model: asymmetric-Laplace (two-piece exponential) family for the daily log return r, f(r; p,a,b) = p a exp(-a r) 1{r>0} + (1-p) b exp(b r) 1{r<0}, with p = P(r>0), 1/a = E[r | r>0], 1/b = E[-r | r<0]. Fisher metric: ds^2 = dp^2/(p(1-p)) + p da^2/a^2 + (1-p) db^2/b^2. MLE on a trailing window of n bars with Krichevsky-Trofimov half counts: p = (n_+ + 1/2)/(n + 1), 1/a = S_+/(n_+ + 1/2), 1/b = S_-/(n_- + 1/2) (S_+- = sums of gains and losses; zero returns split evenly). Mirror involution iota: (p,a,b) -> (1-p,b,a), an isometry of the Fisher metric whose fixed set is the symmetric submanifold M_s = {p=1/2, a=b}. Divergence to the mirror image (KL chain rule = frequency part + magnitude part): D = KL(P || iota P) = (2p-1) ln(p/(1-p)) + p[ln(a/b) + b/a - 1] + (1-p)[ln(b/a) + a/b - 1]. Signed statistic: z_n = sgn(m) sqrt(2 n D), m = p/a - (1-p)/b (model mean). Multi-scale signal: Z_t = [z_{w/2} + z_w + z_{2w}]/3 with w = 20 (windows of 10, 20 and 40 bars). ## s10 wasserstein_shape_stability (optimal_transport)

**Hypothesis.** Long-only risk premia (equity, commodity, currency carry) are earned mainly while the return-generating distribution is stable, and drawdowns cluster in transitions in which the distribution's shape is being reshaped: volatility expanding or collapsing, tails fattening, asymmetry flipping. Such changes are shared across asset classes and are measured directly by the cost of optimally transporting the shape of the recent return distribution onto the shape of the preceding one. When this centred transport cost is low relative to its own recent history the market is in a stable regime and the strategy is long; when it becomes unusually high the regime is in transition and the strategy steps aside until the cost falls back. Removing the location part of the transport makes the signal independent of recent drift, so it is neither a momentum nor a reversal rule: it times exposure by distributional stability alone.

**Definition.** Let r_t be daily log returns. Recent measure mu_t = empirical law of {r_{t-s+1},...,r_t} (s = short = 30); reference measure nu_t = empirical law of {r_{t-s-R+1},...,r_{t-s}} (R = ref = 90), the window immediately preceding it. With empirical quantile functions on the grid u_k = (k-1/2)/40, k=1..40, centred as Q~(u_k) = Q(u_k) - mean_j Q(u_j), the signal is the scale-free centred transport cost D_t = sqrt(mean_k (Q~_mu(u_k) - Q~_nu(u_k))^2) / sqrt(mean_k Q~_nu(u_k)^2). ## s11 gain_loss_transport_asymmetry (optimal_transport)

**Hypothesis.** Within a recent window, split daily returns into the law of gains and the law of loss magnitudes and optimally transport one onto the other. When the transport moves mass predominantly downward (losses are larger than gains at every matched quantile level, independently of how often each occurs), the market carries a negative-asymmetry, crash-risk profile. Investors dislike such negative skewness and require compensation for bearing it (skewness preference), and these episodes often follow forced selling, so holding the asset while the loss law dominates the gain law earns a premium. When the transport moves mass predominantly upward (gains larger than losses: lottery-like, short-covering or bubble-like behaviour), expected returns are lower and the strategy is flat. Because the measure compares the two halves of the same window with each other, and is normalised by the transport cost, it does not depend on the drift relative to an earlier period, on the volatility level, or on the frequency of up versus down days.

**Definition.** Let r_{t-W+1..t} be the last W=30 daily log returns. Gain law mu_G = empirical law of {r_i : r_i > 0}; loss law mu_L = empirical law of {-r_i : r_i < 0} (both on R_+, at least 5 points each, else NaN). In one dimension the optimal transport map from mu_G to mu_L for any convex cost is the monotone rearrangement T = Q_L o F_G, so the displacement at quantile level u is delta(u) = Q_G(u) - Q_L(u), evaluated at u_k = (k - 0.5)/10, k = 1..10 (linear-interpolation empirical quantiles). W1(mu_G, mu_L) = int_0^1 |delta(u)| du ~ mean_k |delta(u_k)|. The signed transport asymmetry is A_t = int delta(u) du / int |delta(u)| du in [-1, 1]: the net share of the transport cost that moves mass up (A=1: gain law first-order stochastically dominates the loss law; A=-1: loss law dominates). Rule: entry when A_t < -0.3, exit when A_t > 0.3 (hysteresis band; the harness holds the long position in between).

## s12 gain_loss_time_transport (optimal_transport)

**Hypothesis.** Inside a recent window, the gains and the losses each form a probability law on the time axis (where in the window the up-moves and the down-moves happened, weighted by their size). Optimally transporting the loss law onto the gain law along time tells whether the market rose and then gave some of it back (loss mass transported toward the present: a pullback) or fell and then recovered (loss mass in the past: the rebound has already happened). Pullbacks after earlier gains are mostly driven by liquidity demand and profit taking rather than new information. Investors who supply liquidity by buying them earn a short-horizon reversal premium until the ordering flips. Once the gains are the most recent part of the window, that premium has already been paid and the strategy steps aside. Each temporal law is normalised by its own total mass, so the index depends only on the timing of gains relative to losses. It does not depend on the net drift of the window, the volatility level or the share of up days. That makes it neither a momentum nor a plain reversal rule on the window return.

**Definition.** Let r_1..r_W be the last W log returns (W = window = 60, i = 1 oldest), tau_i = i/W. Gain mass g_i = max(r_i, 0), loss mass l_i = max(-r_i, 0). Temporal probability laws mu_G = sum_i (g_i / sum_j g_j) delta_{tau_i}, mu_L = sum_i (l_i / sum_j l_j) delta_{tau_i}, with CDFs F_G(tau_k) = sum_{i<=k} g_i / sum g, F_L likewise. On the line the optimal transport cost is W1(mu_L, mu_G) = int_0^1 |F_L(tau) - F_G(tau)| dtau (Vallender), and the signed transport displacement is S = int_0^1 (F_L - F_G) dtau = E_{mu_G}[tau] - E_{mu_L}[tau]. Discretely, W1 = (1/W) sum_k |F_L(tau_k) - F_G(tau_k)|, S = (1/W) sum_k (F_L - F_G)(tau_k). The signal is the transport dominance index D = S / W1 in [-1, 1]. D = +1 iff mu_G first-order dominates mu_L in time, i.e. gains come after losses at every quantile level. D = -1 iff loss mass lies later at every level. D = 0 if a window has no gains, no losses or identical temporal laws. Rule (hysteresis): entry when D_t < -0.25 (loss mass transported toward the present, a pullback), exit when D_t > +0.25 (loss mass in the past); the position is otherwise held.

**Novelty (agent's claim).** The earlier strategies in this field all transport in the space of return values. ## s13 price_qv_levy_area_rotation (rough_paths)

**Hypothesis.** The joint path of price and its own quadratic variation carries information that neither coordinate carries alone: the order in which returns and variance arrive. A strongly negative area marks a price/variance rotation typical of stress (variance building up while the price falls, positively skewed moves being given back, a leverage-effect regime), which tends to come before poor risk-adjusted returns and deep drawdowns. **Definition.** For each bar t take the last W=40 daily log returns r_1..r_W (bars t-W+1..t). Price channel: dX_i = r_i / sqrt(sum_j r_j^2), so X has unit quadratic variation over the window. ## s14 two_scale_pvariation_imbalance (rough_paths)

**Hypothesis.** The p-variation of a price path is a supremum over all partitions, so its optimal partition picks out the path's dominant oscillations (the legs that carry the roughness) independently of any fixed sampling grid or volatility level. Splitting the p-variation into the part carried by up-legs and by down-legs gives a scale-free measure of which direction dominates the path's rough structure. When the dominant legs are up at both a monthly and a yearly scale, the path is in a coherent advance and tends to continue (time-series momentum seen through the path's variation geometry); when the recent path is dominated by large down-legs (a coherent sell-off), subsequent risk-adjusted returns and drawdowns are poor. Holding only while the combined imbalance is positive, and leaving at once when the short-scale imbalance shows a coherent sell-off, should improve Sharpe and drawdown versus being always long, in any market with trending phases.

**Definition.** Let x_0..x_n be the log-price on the last n bars (piecewise-linear path X). For p >= 1, the p-variation is V_p(X) = sup over partitions 0 = t_0 < ... < t_k = n of sum_i |x_{t_i} - x_{t_{i-1}}|^p, attained on vertices and computed exactly by dynamic programming: V(0) = 0, V(j) = max_{i<j} [V(i) + |x_j - x_i|^p], V_p = V(n). Signals: a_t = I over n = 30 bars, b_t = I over n = 252 bars, both with p = 2. Score s_t = a_t + b_t. Entry when s_t > 0; exit when s_t < 0 or a_t < -0.5 (the short-scale path is dominated by down-legs, a coherent sell-off). Long or flat only; the harness applies execution delay and costs.

## s15 multiscale_time_price_levy_area_pullback (rough_paths)

## s16 signature_mmd_regime_filter (rough_paths)

**Hypothesis.** The expected signature of short path segments characterises the law of the price process at that scale (increments, quadratic variation, ordering of moves, higher iterated integrals). A maximum mean discrepancy built on the signature feature map is therefore a model-free two-sample test for 'has the law of recent paths moved away from the law of the trailing reference?'. Most of the time markets are in a locally stationary regime and the long-only holder earns the asset's drift. When the recent path law is anomalous AND the MMD witness points in the adverse direction (the recent mean level-1 signature is below the reference: a sell-off-type change of law, typically with altered quadratic variation and ordering), the subsequent returns are poor and drawdowns deep, so stepping aside until the law returns to normal, or the witness turns favourable, should improve Sharpe and drawdown per unit volatility versus staying long through regime breaks, in any liquid market.

**Definition.** Returns r_t. For each bar s, the segment of the last w=10 returns is embedded as the time-augmented lead-lag path Z = (t, X^lead, X^lag) in R^3 (2w linear pieces: lead step (0, r, 0), then lag+time step (1/w, 0, r)). S(Z)_s is its signature truncated at level 3, computed exactly by Chen's identity: S1+=D, S2+=S1(x)D + D(x)D/2, S3+=S2(x)D + S1(x)D(x)D/2 + D(x)D(x)D/6; the three pure-time words are dropped, leaving k=36 words (level 2 contains the Levy areas and the quadratic variation [X] via the lead-lag area). Calibration: q_t = fraction of in-reference blocks B_u = mean{S_s: u-m+1<=s<=u}, u in [t-L+m, t-m], with D(B_u) < D(C_t) (rank of the current discrepancy against the reference's own block-to-mean variability). ## s17 higuchi_smooth_path_filter (fractal)

## s18 fbm_increment_forecast (fractal)

**Hypothesis.** If log prices behave locally like fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent H, then two adjacent price moves over equal horizons have correlation rho(H) = 2^(2H-1) - 1. By self-similarity this holds at every horizon. So the local H, together with the latest move, gives a conditional forecast of the next move. In persistent regimes (H > 1/2) moves tend to continue; in anti-persistent regimes (H < 1/2) they tend to reverse; near H = 1/2 the past carries no information. A long-only holder should buy when this forecast is clearly positive (a persistent advance, or a sell-off in an anti-persistent market), sell when it is clearly negative (a persistent decline, or a rally in an anti-persistent market), and otherwise keep the current position. The rule therefore follows trends or fades them depending on the measured memory of the market, rather than holding a fixed momentum or reversal bias.

**Definition.** Inputs: daily log returns r_t. (1) Hurst exponent by DFA-1 on the last W=120 returns: profile y_i = sum_{j<=i}(r_j - mean(r)); for box sizes n in {4,6,9,13,20,30} (log-spaced, 4..W/4), cut the profile into floor(W/n) non-overlapping boxes aligned to the latest bar. Remove a least-squares line from each box and take F(n) = sqrt(mean of squared residuals). Finite-size correction: F0(n) is the exact expected DFA fluctuation of i.i.d. unit noise under the same procedure, F0(n)^2 = sum_k tr(M_n S_k M_n') / (K n), where S = C D C' is the null covariance of the profile (C lower-triangular ones, D = I - 11'/W) and M_n = I - X(X'X)^-1X'. H_t is the OLS slope of log F(n) - [log F0(n) - 0.5 log n] on log n, so white noise gives H = 0.5 without small-sample bias. H is clipped to [0.05, 0.95]. (2) Standardised last move over L=40 bars: z_t = sum_{i=t-L+1..t} r_i / (s_t sqrt(L)), where s_t is the sample s.d. of those L returns. (3) fBm conditional forecast: for fBm, Corr(X(t+L)-X(t), X(t)-X(t-L)) = 2^(2H-1) - 1 =: rho(H), so the standardised forecast is f_t = rho(H_t) * z_t. Rule: entry if f_t > 0.05; exit if f_t < -0.10; otherwise no action (hysteresis). Lookback 121 bars.

## s19 holder_singularity_rebound (fractal)

**Hypothesis.** Price drops differ in their local regularity. A drop concentrated in an abrupt, jump-like break at the current point is a singularity of the price path, with a pointwise Hoelder exponent well below the Brownian value 1/2. Such breaks are typically liquidity or overreaction shocks, and they tend to revert. A drop that unfolds regularly, with alpha near or above 1/2, is a diffusive or trending repricing and carries no such rebound premium. A long-only holder should therefore buy only singular drops, stay invested while the dip heals, and step aside once the price has regained its pre-shock high or the shock has aged. This keeps exposure low in driftless markets while capturing the rebound premium where it exists.

**Definition.** x_t = log price. Pointwise Hoelder exponent at bar t (oscillation method): osc_tau(t) = max_{t-tau<=s<=t} x_s - min_{t-tau<=s<=t} x_s for tau = 1..K (K = 10). Since osc_tau(t) ~ C tau^{alpha(t)}, estimate alpha(t) as the OLS slope of log osc_tau(t) on log tau. For Brownian motion alpha = 1/2. For a smooth trend alpha -> 1. For a jump at t, osc_tau ~ const and alpha -> 0. Dip depth in diffusive units: z_t = (x_t - max_{t-h<=s<=t} x_s) / (sigma_t sqrt(h)), with h = 10 and sigma_t the sample std of the last 20 daily log returns. Event E_t = 1{alpha(t) < 0.35 and z_t < -0.5}. Entry condition: E_t. Exit condition: (no event in bars t-29..t) or z_t >= 0 (price back at its 10-bar high). The harness holds the long position from entry to exit.

## s20 fmh_horizon_gap (fractal)

**Hypothesis.** Fractal Markets Hypothesis (Peters 1994; Kristoufek 2012, 2013): a market is stable when traders with many investment horizons coexist, so that price fluctuations obey a single scaling law across horizons. Turbulent, illiquid phases are marked by a breakdown of this self-similarity: the scaling exponent at long horizons rises relative to the one at short horizons (long-horizon moves become persistent, one-sided repricing while short-horizon dynamics are noisy/anti-persistent). A long-only holder should therefore be invested while the long-horizon Hurst exponent does not exceed the short-horizon one by more than is usual for that market, and step aside when the horizon gap is in the upper half of its recent distribution. This cuts exposure in regimes where drawdowns cluster while retaining exposure in balanced, multi-horizon (liquid) regimes.

**Definition.** Let X_t be the log price. Second-order structure function over a trailing window of w bars: S2(tau; t, w) = (1/w) * sum_{s=t-w+1..t} (X_s - X_{s-tau})^2. For self-affine (fBm-like) paths S2(tau) ~ c * tau^(2H). Local scale-restricted Hurst exponents are OLS slopes in log-log coordinates: H_short(t,w) = (1/2) * slope of log S2(tau) on log tau for tau in {1,2,3}; H_long(t,w) = (1/2) * slope for tau in {5,10,20}. Horizon gap: G_t = (1/3) * sum_{w in {2W/3, W, 3W/2}} [H_long(t,w) - H_short(t,w)], W = 60 (windows 40, 60, 90). Under a single scaling law (e.g. fBm with any H) G = 0 in expectation; G > 0 means a crossover to stronger persistence at long horizons. Rule: m_t = median of G over the trailing 126 bars; entry if G_t < m_t, exit if G_t > m_t. Lookback: 90 + 20 bars for G plus 126 for the median.

## s21 kramers_moyal_state_drift (statistical_physics)

**Hypothesis.** Price dynamics can be described as a Langevin process dx = D1(z) dt + sqrt(2 D2(z)) dW whose drift depends on a dimensionless state z (displacement of log price from its local mean in units of local dispersion). The state-dependent drift field D1(z), i.e. the effective force -dU/dz of the landscape the price moves in, is persistent enough over months to be estimated from an instrument's own recent history with finite-time Kramers-Moyal coefficients. Being long only when the estimated drift at the current state is clearly positive relative to the diffusion captures adaptive trend or reversion behaviour (whichever the instrument's recent dynamics shows) and stays flat where the drift field is indistinguishable from zero (driftless, random-walk-like markets).

**Definition.** x_t = log price. State: z_t = (x_t - mean_{60}(x)) / std_{60}(x) over the trailing 60 bars. Finite-time increments dx_s = x_{s+tau} - x_s, tau = 5. Conditional Kramers-Moyal moments: M1(z_t) = sum_s w_s dx_s / sum_s w_s (= tau D1(z_t) + O(tau^2)), M2(z_t) = sum_s w_s dx_s^2 / sum_s w_s (= 2 tau D2(z_t) + O(tau^2)). Signal K_t = M1(z_t) / sqrt(M2(z_t)), a drift-to-diffusion (Peclet-type) ratio. Rule: entry when K_t > 0.10; exit when K_t < 0 (hysteresis band [0, 0.10]). Total lookback 60 + 252 + 5 + 1 = 318 bars.

## s22 fluctuation_theorem_ness_dip (statistical_physics)

**Hypothesis.** Price increments are the time-integrated current of a driven stochastic system. Over about a year an instrument is either close to equilibrium (detailed balance, time-reversible increments, zero entropy production) or in a non-equilibrium steady state (NESS) with a persistent current. The fluctuation theorem implies that, in a NESS with positive entropy production Sigma, negative fluctuations of the integrated current are exponentially suppressed (P(-A)/P(+A) = exp(-Sigma A)). They still occur but are transient and relax back along the current. Buying short negative fluctuations only while the estimated entropy production is positive, and leaving once they have relaxed or once the current disappears, harvests the steady-state drift with low exposure and avoids equilibrium (driftless) or reversed-current regimes.

**Definition.** Daily log returns r_t, log price p_t.
1) Entropy production (FT symmetry function). Over the last L=252 bars standardise a_i = r_i / sd(r). Under the fluctuation theorem ln[P(+a)/P(-a)] = Sigma*a, so P(sign a_i = + | |a_i|) = 1/(1+exp(-Sigma |a_i|)). Sigma_hat is the maximum-likelihood estimate (8 Newton steps on the concave log-likelihood sum_i [y_i ln p_i + (1-y_i) ln(1-p_i)], y_i = 1{a_i>0}). Its Fisher information is I = sum_i p_i(1-p_i) a_i^2 and the test statistic is z_t = Sigma_hat * sqrt(I), with z ~ N(0,1) under detailed balance (Sigma = 0).
2) Current fluctuation: d_t = (p_t - p_{t-tau}) / (sqrt(tau) * T_t), tau=5, with local temperature T_t = sqrt(mean_{60 bars} r^2).
Rule: entry when z_t > 0 and d_t < -0.5 (negative fluctuation inside a positive-current NESS); exit when d_t > 1.0 (fluctuation relaxed and overshot) or z_t < -0.5 (current reversed). Long otherwise held; flat otherwise.

## s23 soft_spin_fluctuation_relaxation (statistical_physics)

**Hypothesis.** Treat the sequence of daily returns as a chain of soft Ising spins at a slowly varying temperature. Superstatistics: returns are locally Gaussian with a fluctuating temperature, so each return is scaled by the local temperature before being squashed into a bounded spin. The block magnetization over two weeks is the order parameter of the 'market mood'. Its statistics over the previous six months define the current equilibrium state: the mean is the field-polarised equilibrium magnetization, and the dispersion is the equilibrium fluctuation scale, i.e. the empirical susceptibility. Onsager's regression hypothesis and the fluctuation-dissipation theorem say that spontaneous fluctuations away from equilibrium decay on the same time scale and the same way as an externally imposed perturbation. If markets have a restoring response at the 1-2 week scale (effective anti-ferromagnetic coupling between successive blocks of spins), a large collective down-fluctuation of the order parameter is partly undone by subsequent up spins, not merely diluted. Being long only during the fast initial part of this relaxation, after a fluctuation larger than one equilibrium standard deviation and until it has relaxed to within half a standard deviation, harvests the restoring response with low exposure. Because the scale comes from each instrument's own equilibrium, the rule transfers unchanged across asset classes and volatility regimes.

**Definition.** Inputs: daily log returns r_t.
1. Local temperature (superstatistics): T_t = sqrt( (1/20) * sum_{k=0..19} r_{t-k}^2 ).
2. Soft spins (bounded, tail-robust): s_t = tanh(r_t / T_t), in (-1, 1).
3. Order parameter (block magnetization, W = 10): m_t = (1/W) * sum_{k=0..W-1} s_{t-k}.
4. Equilibrium ensemble over the trailing L = 120 values of m:
   <m>_t = mean(m_{t-L+1..t}),   chi_t = std(m_{t-L+1..t}) (block susceptibility,
   W * Var(m) = chi * T in the fluctuation-dissipation sense).
5. Fluctuation in equilibrium units: zeta_t = (m_t - <m>_t) / chi_t.
Rule: entry when zeta_t < -1 (spontaneous fluctuation against equilibrium of at least one equilibrium std); exit when zeta_t > -0.5 (fluctuation relaxed to within half a std). The position is long from entry to exit. Because the down-spins that caused the fluctuation leave the W-bar block after at most 10 bars, the exit is reached either by restoring up-spins (a profitable relaxation) or by natural decay of the block. So holding periods are short (typically under two weeks) without any explicit stop. Total lookback: 20 + 10 + 120 = 150 bars.

## s24 negative_bubble_singularity (statistical_physics)

## s25 ssa_residual_reversion (spectral)

**Hypothesis.** Daily log prices are well described over a ~3-month window by a slow component, the leading eigentriple of the SSA (singular-spectrum) decomposition, plus higher-frequency components. Excursions carried by the high-frequency part are transient: when the price falls well below its leading SSA component, it tends to come back to it and overshoot. A long-only position bought on negative residual excursions and held until a positive overshoot captures this reversion and, in assets with positive drift, the drift as well, while spending roughly half the time out of the market.

**Definition.** For each bar t take the trailing window x_{t-L+1..t} of log prices (L = 60), centre it, s_i = x_i - mean(x). Rule: entry when z_t < -1; exit when z_t > +1 (position held from entry until exit; harness applies the one-bar delay).

**Novelty (agent's claim).** Uses the singular-spectrum (eigen-) decomposition of the lagged price trajectory matrix, rather than a moving average, to define the adaptive 'fair' path: the trend is the data-driven leading eigentriple, so its effective frequency response adapts to each window. Trading is on the standardised high-frequency residual of that decomposition, with an asymmetric hold (enter at -1 sd, exit only on a +1 sd overshoot) instead of a symmetric return-to-mean exit.

**Limitations.** Rank-1 SSA endpoint estimates suffer from the usual end effect (the last reconstructed point uses a single trajectory-matrix element), so the residual at the last bar is noisy. In persistent bear markets the strategy repeatedly buys dips that keep falling (several equity segments in the development data); there is no stop. Results were sensitive to the window length (60 clearly better than 40, 90 or 120 in development) and to the exit level, and per-instrument results vary a lot between neighbouring settings, so out-of-sample performance may be noticeably lower. Some FX pairs without mean reversion at this horizon lose money.

## s26 bridge_fourier_phase (spectral)

**Hypothesis.** Over a window of about one quarter (60 trading days), the log price moves as a drift plus a slow swing around that drift. The fundamental Fourier component of the drift-free part of the window (the Brownian-bridge residual) summarises the swing, and its phase at the last bar shows where on the swing the price now is. Buying just after the swing has turned up from its trough and selling just after it has turned down from its peak captures the rising half of the oscillation. Being out of the market for about half the time, mostly during falling phases, also cuts drawdowns compared with buy and hold.

**Definition.** For each bar t take the last n = 60 log prices x_0..x_{n-1} (x_{n-1} = log price at t). Remove the chord through the two end points: y_j = x_j - [x_0 + (x_{n-1} - x_0) j/(n-1)], so y_0 = y_{n-1} = 0 (Brownian-bridge detrending, which avoids the end-point jump that would otherwise leak into all frequencies of the DFT). Fundamental DFT coefficient: X_1 = sum_j y_j exp(-2 pi i j / n), with amplitude |X_1| and phase arg X_1. The k=1 harmonic is (2/n)|X_1| cos(2 pi j/n + arg X_1); its phase at the last sample is theta_t = arg X_1 + 2 pi (n-1)/n. Signals: cycle_cos = cos theta_t, cycle_sin = sin theta_t. Rule: entry when cos theta_t < 0 and sin theta_t < 0 (theta in (pi, 3pi/2): past the trough, harmonic rising); exit when cos theta_t > 0 and sin theta_t > 0 (theta in (0, pi/2): past the peak, harmonic falling). The two quadrants are not adjacent, which gives hysteresis; the position is otherwise held. Lookback: 60 bars.

**Signals.**

- `cycle_cos` on log_price; window=Window(60); lookback 60
- `cycle_sin` on log_price; window=Window(60); lookback 60

Rule lookback 0; total 60 bars.

**References.**

- Granger, C.W.J. and Morgenstern, O. (1963). Spectral analysis of New York stock market prices. Kyklos 16(1), 1-27.
- Priestley, M.B. (1981). Spectral Analysis and Time Series. Academic Press, London.
- Percival, D.B. and Walden, A.T. (1993). Spectral Analysis for Physical Applications: Multitaper and Conventional Univariate Techniques. Cambridge University Press.
- Poterba, J.M. and Summers, L.H. (1988). Mean reversion in stock prices: evidence and implications. Journal of Financial Economics 22(1), 27-59.
- Ehlers, J.F. (2001). Rocket Science for Traders: Digital Signal Processing Applications. Wiley, New York.

**Novelty (agent's claim).** This rule trades on the phase of one Fourier harmonic, not on the size of a deviation. ssa_residual_reversion measures how far the price sits below a data-adaptive SSA trend (an eigen-decomposition of the trajectory matrix) and trades that excursion against a threshold. Here the trend is the fixed end-point chord, the decomposition is the fixed Fourier basis, and the decision depends only on the angle of the fundamental component (where the bar sits on the cycle and which way it is moving). There is no amplitude threshold, so the rule is scale-free across assets and volatility regimes. Exits happen after the cycle peak, not on a positive overshoot.

**Limitations.** The phase of a single harmonic is a noisy estimate, and results depend clearly on the window: on the development data, 60 bars scored about 0.30, 90 bars about 0.12 and 40 bars was negative. Under a pure random walk the phase carries no information, so the edge depends on real quarter-scale oscillations. The rule ignores the chord slope, so it keeps buying troughs during persistent bear markets (weak equity segments late in the sample). With a 60-bar window it enters about four times a year, so trading costs and the 2-entries-per-year minimum matter for longer windows. It does worst on some currency pairs with no drift.

## s27 spectral_entropy_coherence_gate (spectral)

**Hypothesis.** When daily returns over the last ~2 months are spectrally noise-like (power spread evenly over frequencies, high spectral entropy), the market is in an ordinary, unstructured regime in which a long position earns the asset's drift premium. When the spectrum becomes concentrated in a few frequencies (low spectral entropy) the price path is driven by an organised, persistent component; if that coherent episode is a decline (negative window drift), it tends to carry on and to concentrate drawdowns, so being flat during it reduces drawdown relative to volatility more than it costs in return. Re-entering only once the spectrum has returned to a noise-like state avoids buying into the middle of the organised move.

**Definition.** Let r_t be daily log returns, N = window (default 40), L = long_window (default 120). For each t take x_n = r_{t-N+1+n} - mean, n = 0..N-1, apply the Hann taper w_n = 0.5(1 - cos(2*pi*n/(N-1))), and compute the periodogram P_k = |sum_n w_n x_n exp(-2*pi*i*k*n/N)|^2 for k = 1..floor(N/2) (DC excluded). Normalise p_k = P_k / sum_j P_j and define the normalised spectral entropy H_t = -sum_k p_k ln p_k / ln(K), K = floor(N/2) (H = 1 for a flat/white spectrum). Signal 1 (entropy_rank): Q_t = (1/L) * #{s in [t-L+1, t] : H_s < H_t}, a causal percentile rank that makes the threshold self-calibrating for every market. Signal 2 (window_drift): D_t = sum_{j=0}^{N-1} r_{t-j} (the zero-frequency Fourier coefficient of the window). Rule: entry when Q_t > 0.5; exit when Q_t < 0.3 and D_t < 0. The harness holds from entry to exit (exit wins on ties) with its own one-bar delay and costs. Total lookback 160 bars.

**Signals.**

- `entropy_rank` on log_return; window=Window(40), long_window=LongWindow(120); lookback 160
- `window_drift` on log_return; window=Window(40); lookback 40

Rule lookback 0; total 160 bars.

**References.**

- Powell, G.E. and Percival, I.C. (1979). A spectral entropy method for distinguishing regular and irregular motion of Hamiltonian systems. Journal of Physics A: Mathematical and General, 12(11), 2053-2071.
- Inouye, T., Shinosaki, K., Sakamoto, H., Toi, S., Ukai, S., Iyama, A., Katsuda, Y. and Hirano, M. (1991). Quantification of EEG irregularity by use of the entropy of the power spectrum. Electroencephalography and Clinical Neurophysiology, 79(3), 204-210.
- Harris, F.J. (1978). On the use of windows for harmonic analysis with the discrete Fourier transform. Proceedings of the IEEE, 66(1), 51-83.
- Percival, D.B. and Walden, A.T. (1993). Spectral Analysis for Physical Applications: Multitaper and Conventional Univariate Techniques. Cambridge University Press.

**Novelty (agent's claim).** Uses the shape of the whole power distribution (its Shannon entropy) as a regime detector, not any particular component. Unlike ssa_residual_reversion it does not decompose the price into a trend plus residual or trade reversion of a residual. Unlike bridge_fourier_phase it does not use the phase of a fundamental harmonic or try to time a cycle. Here the frequency domain only says whether the market is unstructured (stay long) or organised (step aside if the organised move is down). The strategy is long about 65-80% of the time and exits only during spectrally coherent declines.

**Limitations.** The edge on the development data was small: the objective was 0.084, and nearby parameter settings scored between about -0.12 and +0.08, so the result is within sampling noise of a near-buy-and-hold profile. A 40-bar periodogram is a noisy spectral estimate; a multitaper estimate did not help. Being mostly long, it earns little on driftless markets such as currency pairs, where it scores negative because of drawdowns, and on markets with persistent negative drift. It reacts with a lag of several weeks, so it does not avoid sudden one-day crashes, which spread power over all frequencies and therefore have high entropy. On some instruments the number of entries per year is close to the minimum of 2 required by the evaluation.

## s28 long_memory_scaled_dip (spectral)

**Hypothesis.** The behaviour of the return spectrum near frequency zero shows whether multi-week price moves tend to persist or to revert. If the low-frequency spectrum of daily returns falls towards zero frequency (memory parameter d < 0, anti-persistent returns, so the price mean-reverts at horizons of weeks), a short-term decline is likely to be partly undone, and even a modest dip is worth buying. If the spectrum rises towards zero frequency (d > 0, persistent returns), declines tend to continue, and only an unusually deep, capitulation-like dip should be bought. Making the required dip depth a continuous function of d, instead of an on/off regime switch, keeps the strategy active in every regime. It buys reversion where the spectrum supports it and avoids catching falling knives where the spectrum indicates persistence. Holding until a modest overshoot (+0.5 sigma) captures the rebound plus the asset's drift while being flat roughly half the time.

**Definition.** Let r_t be daily log returns. (1) Memory parameter: over the last N=252 returns (demeaned), compute the sine-multitaper spectrum S(l_k) = (1/K) sum_{j=1..K} |sum_t h_j(t) r_t exp(-i l_k t)|^2 with K=3 sine tapers h_j(t)=sqrt(2/(N+1)) sin(pi j t/(N+1)) and Fourier frequencies l_k = 2 pi k/N. Using the m=20 lowest frequencies (k=1..m, periods of about 12.6 to 252 days; m = round(1.25 sqrt(N))), estimate d by the Geweke-Porter-Hudak log-periodogram regression log S(l_k) = c + d * X_k + e_k, with X_k = -log(4 sin^2(l_k/2)), so that S(l) ~ l^(-2d) near zero (d = OLS slope). (2) Dip: z_t = (r_{t-9}+...+r_t) / (sigma_t sqrt(10)), where sigma_t is the sample standard deviation of the last 120 returns. (3) Rule: entry when z_t < -0.5 - 5 d_t (white noise d=0: dip below -0.5 sigma; anti-persistent d=-0.1: any negative 10-day move; persistent d=+0.1: dip below -1 sigma). Exit when z_t > +0.5. Long only, flat otherwise. Total lookback is 252 bars.

**Signals.**

- `gph_d` on log_return; window=LongWindow(252), band=Kmax(8); lookback 252
- `dip_z` on log_return; window=Window(10), vol_window=LongWindow(120); lookback 120

Rule lookback 0; total 252 bars.

**References.**

- Geweke, J. and Porter-Hudak, S. (1983). The estimation and application of long memory time series models. Journal of Time Series Analysis 4(4), 221-238.
- Robinson, P. M. (1995). Log-periodogram regression of time series with long range dependence. Annals of Statistics 23(3), 1048-1072.
- Riedel, K. S. and Sidorenko, A. (1995). Minimum bias multiple taper spectral estimation. IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing 43(1), 188-195.
- Velasco, C. (1999). Non-stationary log-periodogram regression. Journal of Econometrics 91(2), 325-371.
- Hosking, J. R. M. (1981). Fractional differencing. Biometrika 68(1), 165-176.
- Percival, D. B. and Walden, A. T. (1993). Spectral Analysis for Physical Applications: Multitaper and Conventional Univariate Techniques. Cambridge University Press.
- Lo, A. W. and MacKinlay, A. C. (1988). Stock market prices do not follow random walks: evidence from a simple specification test. Review of Financial Studies 1(1), 41-66.

**Novelty (agent's claim).** The earlier spectral strategies use the leading SSA component as a reversion anchor, the phase of the fundamental Fourier mode of a Brownian-bridge residual, or the spectral entropy (flatness) of the whole spectrum. This strategy instead uses the low-frequency power-law exponent of the return spectrum: the fractional memory parameter d, estimated by multitaper GPH log-periodogram regression. d does not decide whether to trade. It continuously sets how deep a short-term dip has to be before buying, so the spectral shape near zero frequency becomes a horizon-matched oversold threshold. No price anchor, phase or entropy is involved.

**Limitations.** The GPH estimate from 252 bars and 20 frequencies is noisy (standard error about 0.07 to 0.1 with 3 tapers), so the threshold modulation is partly random. The linear map from d to the threshold (base -0.5, slope 5) is heuristic, not derived from a forecast model. The strategy relies on short-horizon reversion plus drift. On currency pairs without drift it was roughly break-even to slightly negative in development (negative F on most FX pairs), and on indices in persistent bear phases it keeps buying dips that fail. Its turnover is fairly high (about 15 to 25 one-way trades a year on equities), so it is sensitive to costs. Development objective: -0.04 (median F over 21 instruments); mean F +0.05. Neighbouring parameter settings scored between -0.13 and -0.04.
